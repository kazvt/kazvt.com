# Ramble Gamble menu karaoke

The owner-supplied `ramble gamble 2026-11-28 0626.mp3` is unchanged at `assets/music/ramble-gamble-theme.mp3` (198.288 s; SHA-256 `7485aa4feaf5557e2688b1ecf4fe448d96ec7e33d2388842096cd4d319258ec5`). The 240 whitespace-delimited lyric tokens are retained exactly, including all backing responses and the scat ending; the long last line is split into three readable phrases. Opening, instrumental and outro have an instrumental note display, not prematurely displayed lyrics.

The replacement mix has the same decoded duration and no detected sample shift in 19 five-second waveform windows, sampled every ten seconds. The revised cues use direct passes on the final mix; matching duration alone does not verify individual vocal onsets.

## Timing confidence — read before treating this as finished karaoke

The cue module is **revised but provisional**, not a verified vocal transcription. Local PocketSphinx speech recognition, constrained lyric grammars on raw and center/recurrence-filtered audio, and spectral recurrence/beat analysis were used. The recording has a strong approximately 92 BPM period and recurrent musical passages separated by about 41.74 s. Some phrase/word onsets were recoverable, particularly in the opening, but most sung words (and especially scat) could not be reliably forced-aligned; the strict whole-song grammar failed. Unavailable model weights and disabled network downloads prevented use of a stronger singing/vocal-alignment model. Several word boundaries are interpolated from provisional phrase intervals and syllable weights. The revised pass compares mid/left/right/upper-band audio and phase-conditioned accompaniment masks against the known lyrics. Opening onsets agree around 1.81, 4.29, 4.40, 4.85, 5.10, 5.81, 7.08 and 7.45 s. The first and second chorus fourth-line anchors show a 41.74 s recurrence (e.g. “along” 20.73 and 62.47 s). The third chorus had an approximately 135.65 s musical recurrence and its fourth line had direct candidate onsets around 153.2–153.4 s rather than the previous 158.1 s. The revised sheet uses 153.4 s for that line and 159.67 s for the scat entrance. Recurrence is a musical cue, not proof of lyric timing. Some other line boundaries were shortened/reallocated to maintain order; first hook, remaining choruses and scat word times still require listening-based correction. The cue sheet may require substantial per-phrase or per-word adjustment. Browser tests verify synchronization to the cue sheet, not its acoustic correctness. Do not claim perfect lyric alignment.

## Automatic acoustic alignment (offline authoring, optional)

The latest delay was not a verified word-by-word alignment. A fresh PocketSphinx run on the actual final MP3 (including a center-filtered version) did not recover reliable chorus anchors, and network/model downloads are unavailable in the analysis environment. The existing cue sheet is therefore deliberately left unchanged in this tooling patch. Do not treat an unverified timing estimate as a correction.

Run on an Internet-connected machine with Python 3.10–3.12, FFmpeg and sufficient RAM, from the project root. This installs only an isolated authoring environment, not a website dependency. On Windows, the Faster-Whisper Python package and its `small.en` model are already available in this machine's Python 3.10/Hugging Face cache, so use a virtual environment that can reuse those installed packages:

```powershell
py -3.10 -m venv --system-site-packages "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\RambleGambleKaraoke\venv"
& "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\RambleGambleKaraoke\venv\Scripts\python.exe" -m pip install "stable-ts[fw]"
& "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\RambleGambleKaraoke\venv\Scripts\python.exe" ramblegamble\tools\align-menu-karaoke.py --backend faster-whisper --model small.en --device cuda --output "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\RambleGambleKaraoke\alignment"
```

For other environments, install `ffmpeg` and `python3-venv` if needed on Debian/Ubuntu, then:

```sh
python3 -m venv .karaoke-venv
.karaoke-venv/bin/python -m pip install stable-ts
.karaoke-venv/bin/python ramblegamble/tools/align-menu-karaoke.py --model medium.en --device cpu
```

If memory is limited, use `--model small.en`; for a compatible GPU, `--model large-v3 --device cuda`. The model downloads on first run. If the mix masks the voices, optionally separate the original MP3 with Demucs in a compatible environment and pass the untrimmed, time-aligned vocal stem with `--vocals /path/to/vocals.wav`. Do not use a differently edited mix; the tool verifies the bundled MP3 hash and checks stem duration, but duration alone cannot establish the stem's sample origin.

The tool runs unprompted speech-to-text and then forced word alignment of the exact 240 lyric tokens, preserves their original spelling, reconstructs the instrumental intervals from the aligned words, and writes raw `transcription.json`, `forced-alignment.json`, `review.json`, and (only if structural validation passes) `candidate.json` under the requested output directory. `--backend faster-whisper` reuses an existing Faster-Whisper model cache; `--backend openai` uses the standard Whisper backend. It rejects collapsed known instrumental breaks, missing/overlapping words and a mismatched source recording. `review.json` lists independently recognized words within 0.6 s, low probabilities and unusually long words for each phrase. These are diagnostics, not proof of synchronization; sung backing vocals, repeated phrases and the scat ending can confuse Whisper. If validation fails, retain the raw JSON for diagnosis; no live cue changes are made.

Open `/ramblegamble/tools/menu-karaoke-timing.html`, choose **Import Whisper candidate**, select `candidate.json`, listen and verify flagged phrases, then **Apply timing to this browser** and reload the game. Use the editor export button for distributing the verified timings. The import checks the recording identity and the exact lyrics/sections; it never applies automatically. Source: https://github.com/jianfch/stable-ts (documented `load_model`, `load_faster_whisper`, `transcribe`, and `align`). The conversion and validation logic was tested locally against synthetic word timestamps; an alignment candidate must still be reviewed by listening before use.

## Calibrate without changing gameplay

Open `/ramblegamble/tools/menu-karaoke-timing.html` on your existing deployed or local HTTP server. Choose a phrase, listen from one second before it, use 0.5×/0.75× speed if needed, and tap each word onset in order, then the phrase end. Each tap closes the previous word. Edit individual onset/end values or neighbouring phrase boundaries, and check the live word preview. Export the validated `menu-karaoke-cues.mjs` and replace the existing `ramblegamble/menu-karaoke-cues.mjs`. Increment the cue module query version in `menu-karaoke.mjs` and propagate the import cache chain (or hard refresh for local testing). Alternatively, click **Apply timing to this browser** and reload the game; the validated override is scoped to the final recording and this browser/origin. Remove browser override restores bundled timing. Shift phrase moves all selected word and phrase times together. Export to distribute across browsers. No server or SQL storage is involved. The editor is a separate static tool, not linked from the host controls; it can write a local download or an explicitly requested localStorage override. It is not a new authentication boundary.

## Runtime design

`createMenuMarquee` owns the overlay lifecycle. Lyrics are rendered only after the original opening clip hands off to the actually playing menu MP3, or the existing fallback sign settles. `theme.currentTime` is the single clock; the menu's existing RAF drives word selection, progressive yellow lead/blue response fill, inner-ink squash/stretch, a single bouncing ball and two reusable music-note particles (alternating one- and two-note bursts per active lyric word). No second music player for karaoke, media timer, runtime ASR, new external assets, SQL, or WebGL pass. The separate credits-only audio player exclusively replaces menu audio while its dialog is open. The menu's established lock, controls, hidden tab, page suspension, mute, Play/rewind, error and native loop paths are preserved. A muted but advancing theme still displays lyrics. Reduced-motion settings remove particles, the moving ball and squash/stretch while preserving word highlighting. Forced-colors keeps text emphasis. Instrumental passages display only two static music notes; the renderer stops updating when the menu is inactive. Invalid timing or absent markup disables karaoke without breaking Play.

Reference: W3C Media Timed Events (https://www.w3.org/TR/media-timed-events/), MDN HTMLMediaElement.currentTime (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLMediaElement/currentTime), and MDN prefers-reduced-motion (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion).

## Retro presentation

The overlay floats above the menu without a visible panel, border, header, next-line label/preview or progress bar. Unplayed words are white with heavy black outlines; lead vocals fill saturated yellow and backing responses fill electric blue. The ball lands at the active word onset and travels in an arc toward the next word on the same visual row. For wrapped rows or backing responses it restarts at the correct word rather than crossing text. Only the inner word ink squashes and rebounds; the stable outer boxes retain spacing and are measured on cue changes/resize, not every frame. Long-word lateral overshoot is bounded. The existing final recording, timing sheet and recording-scoped calibration key are unchanged; this is a styling patch, not a new acoustic alignment.
