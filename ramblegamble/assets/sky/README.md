# Floating sky video

The floating sky is a silent, 20-second, 20 FPS pre-rendered loop. The game only loads `floating-sky-matte.mp4` (1536×1536 packed RGB and alpha), and only when an unlocked active room camera can see an overhead tile. The separately included `floating-sky-alpha.webm` is the 768×1536 VP9 alpha export (two vertically stacked tiles) for reuse in other tooling. The native alpha export is **not** requested at runtime. `sky-preview.png` is an offline transparent first-frame reference. Both videos contain no audio track.

The atlas has two 768×768 color cells stacked vertically on the left and their matching alpha cells stacked on the right. The shader selects the corresponding row for each tile, with half-texel insets on both axes, compression noise rejection, edge feathering and distance fade. The MP4 approach avoids relying on browser-specific transparent-video upload support. All visible planes share one VideoTexture and geometry, with two reusable shader materials selecting the respective cast. A world-fixed 36×36-unit tile repeats on a 30.24-unit grid; adjacent tiles overlap by 16%, and stable quarter-turn variation and a world-fixed checkerboard of the two distinct casts reduce obvious repetition. Neighbors never use the same cast; diagonal and more distant tiles can still repeat. The packed atlas contains 12.5% more decoded pixels than the previous 2048×1024 frame, not two video players. Each cast is downsampled from 1024×1024 with premultiplied alpha to 768×768 to limit decoder cost. The plane rests at Y=22.5 (1.5× the previous 15). A continuous quadratic knee starts lifting when camera Y exceeds 14.5, then reaches a soft target of camera Y + 6. Exponential damping (2.6 s⁻¹ rising, 1.4 s⁻¹ falling) creates visible lag; the hard guard `max(22.5, camera.y + 3)` is applied after smoothing every frame, even for a camera teleport. The height has no maximum and returns to exactly 22.5 after descent. World X/Z remain fixed. A long frame is capped at 0.25 s for the smoothing step only; hard clearance is never capped. The renderer reuses a 7×7 pool, frustum/distance culls each tile, and pauses the single decoder when no candidate is visible, the room is hidden/locked/paused, or VIP Dice is open. Height smoothing reference: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MathUtils.html and https://www.rorydriscoll.com/2016/03/07/frame-rate-independent-damping-using-lerp/ . Asset variation reference: https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art . No new interactive targets, live model loading, physical simulation, audio, database or runtime library.

## Artwork and provenance

- **Density is 17 bodies per tile**, not 34. Two independent 17-body casts share the same atlas. Each contains 14 ordinary props, two eye spheres and one inscription. The ordinary prop pool is 28 entries (two original stage card artworks share a card silhouette), with 14 genuinely new model designs in cast B; cast A also replaces three previous recolor-only siblings with different silhouettes (open notebook, van, reclining cat). Adjacent tile casts never repeat the same ordinary props. The count of simulated bodies across both baked variants is 34, not the population of an individual tile.
- Retro chair, table, desk, notebooks, cats, cars and sailboat are **locally generated** low-poly models. The “ILY LILLIE” inscription is layered extruded lettering. Both 17-body layouts use eight deterministic material classes (wood, fabric, paper, metal, rubber, fur, glass and paint), stable local face UVs, material grain, weave, scratches, mottling and directional shading. These are procedural surface maps, not photographic scans or a full PBR renderer. Authored card/dice art is unchanged. Original 17-object body radii are restored. New objects are locally built mesh components in `sky_models.py`: rotary telephone, CRT television, cassette, arcade cabinet, lamp, scooter, plane, submarine, teapot, umbrella, shopping cart, guitar, armchair and locomotive. Both casts preserve procedural texturing and the stage card/die textures. All rendering is offline with per-pixel depth and back-face culling.
- Four obsidian spheres have continuously rotating procedural marbling, branching red scleral veins, radial iris filaments, specular highlights, purple motes, and discrete 5 FPS blinking. Their eye apertures and centered pupils always face downward (toward the offline view), independently of the shell’s rotation. They are **stylized, not photographic/hyperrealistic**. No real eye photo has been downloaded or used.
- The offline bake runs a 120 Hz zero-gravity 3D contact solver. Broad-phase radii and AABBs only reject candidates; they do not generate contacts. Disconnected mesh components are separated. Closed convex pieces retain all extremal mesh vertices (no directional support reduction); concave/open pieces are tested against individual authored triangle surfaces with a 0.6-pixel total contact thickness. The text uses merged occupied alpha-pixel runs to retain letter counters and gaps. A vectorized piece-level AABB filter limits narrow-phase SAT tests. Off-center restitution and friction impulses change translation and angular velocity; corrected world geometry is refreshed for both bodies before later pair tests. Ordinary bodies start with components in ±1.65 rad/s (previously ±0.8), with a 0.42 rad/s minimum and 4.2 rad/s maximum. The two independent simulations produce 28 and 23 collision impulses across 13 and 16 distinct pairs respectively. The inscription tumbles more visibly within ±20° pitch, ±18° yaw and ±28° roll, retaining the original timing and a predominantly downward-facing front for readability; the four eye apertures still face down independently of their faster shell rotation. This remains a discrete solver with approximate support-point contact positions, thin surface prisms, a faceted sphere collider and no continuous time-of-impact search or guaranteed solid containment for arbitrary open imported meshes. Depth-separated objects can overlap in projection without physically touching. A shared depth buffer handles foreground/background overlaps. Each 20-second cast runs forward with a two-second premultiplied-alpha dissolve at the loop boundary; brief ghosted silhouettes during the dissolve are intentional. Nothing is simulated in the browser.

## Optional offline re-bake with downloaded assets

No re-bake is required to deploy this patch. On an Internet-connected machine, download models and textures with appropriate licenses and keep their attribution/license notices. Export chosen models as GLBs with embedded textures and conventional Y-up coordinates, named `chair.glb`, `chair2.glb`, `desk.glb`, `table.glb`, `book.glb`, `book2.glb`, `car.glb`, `car2.glb`, `boat.glb`, `cat.glb`, `cat2.glb` in a local directory. The script uses only files that exist, falling back to the supplied art for others. A manually cropped, appropriately licensed close-up eye photo can be supplied via `--eye`; it is mapped into the blinking aperture. The photo override is not verified against an actual photograph in this environment. Choose a crop centered on the eye, without extraneous skin or identifying context. The source eye and model files are not required by the deployed site. The script supports original `card1`, `card2`, `die` overrides as well, but keep the supplied stage art if desired.

Install Python packages in a local virtual environment (`numpy scipy pillow opencv-python-headless trimesh`), FFmpeg with libx264/libvpx-vp9, and the DejaVu Sans font package. The renderer uses `/usr/share/fonts/truetype/dejavu/DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf` for the inscription. From the project root:

```sh
python3 ramblegamble/assets/sky/render-sky.py --models /path/to/glbs --eye /path/to/cropped-eye.jpg
```

Use `--preview-only` to inspect the first rendered frame before encoding, and `--output /path/to/directory` to avoid replacing the shipped files. When replacing the deployed clip, keep the packed format/dimensions and alpha convention or update `floating-sky.mjs` accordingly; bump the video cache version. New model keys in `sky_models.py` also accept a local `<name>.glb` override. The source assets are used only at build time. Offline regression tests: `python3 -m unittest discover -s ramblegamble/assets/sky -p test_sky_physics.py`. No SQL changes.
